Basic instructions
  1. Enter a Hebrew word. For separate names, enter a space between them, for example ישראל מאיר. Up to 6 words are checked in the same area, each with its own skip. Choose As one continuous sequence to join the words instead. Nekudos never count as letters.
  2. Tap Find patterns. Choose Minimum skip and Maximum skip on the main form, or leave the defaults at 1 and 100. These limits apply to each word. Enter 50 in both boxes to search only skip 50. Direction defaults to forward and backward.
  3. The result shows Every 50 letters, for example. This means move 50 letters each time, with 49 letters between the matching letters.
  4. Read First matched letter and Last matched letter for the sefer, perek and pasuk. The list below shows the pasuk for every matching letter.
  5. Read all matching pesukim below the grid. Turn on Show pesukim in between to read the whole passage. Tap a letter in the list to jump to its pasuk. Use Choose another match to compare results.

Forward follows the Torah’s letter order. Backward reads the letters in reverse order. A skip of 1 means adjacent letters, with none between. The example תורה uses a skip of 50 from Bereishis 1:1 to 1:5.

How it works & search limits

For example, in אבגדהוז, starting at א and moving 2 letters each time gives אגהז. In the Torah example, תורה begins at Bereishis 1:1, letter 6, with a skip of +50.

Enter 2 to 32 Hebrew letters per word, with up to 6 separate words. Nearby searches keep areas containing every word, showing one match per word in each area rather than every possible combination. Skips can be 1 to 10,000 in either direction. Each search allows up to 50 million candidate checks, 15 seconds of worker search time, and 500 displayed matches. A stopped search is marked incomplete. Narrow the range to search further. Searches run on your device in a background worker.

These are letter patterns. Finding one does not establish a prediction, hidden message, or statistical significance. The text is joined across words and pesukim; an entire-Torah search can also cross between sefarim.

Torah text, edition & accuracy

Standard Ashkenazi & Sephardi Torah scroll spelling, 304,805 Hebrew letters. This is a written-letter (ketiv) projection of Miqra according to the Masorah, Hebrew Wikisource, using its explicitly documented Ashkenazi and Sephardi scroll readings. The 9 spelling choices below come from that same edition’s notes. Chabad and Yemenite scrolls have some differences; this search uses the standard selection shown here.

The dataset is pinned to MAM-basics f948233b (23 Nov 2026 snapshot). Pasuk references follow BHS Hebrew chapter and verse numbering, including its divisions of the Aseres Hadibros. Other editions can divide those pesukim differently.

Only letters א through ת count. No spaces, vowel or cantillation marks, punctuation, marginal qere readings, section markers, or inverted-nun symbols enter the search. Large and small letters count once. Final letters stay exactly as written in the grid; the optional final-letter setting only changes how comparisons are made. No spelling is inferred from a word’s modern spelling.

Every letter maps to its sefer, perek, pasuk, letter position within that pasuk, and character offset in the selected source text. Tap a grid letter to inspect it. The data file and searchable letter stream are checked against SHA-256 fingerprints before searching.
