How does the countdown work?

Whole months follow the selected calendar (English or Hebrew). Remaining days are split into weeks and days. Under one month, hours and minutes also appear. Zero month, week and day units disappear. A day here is 24 hours; daylight saving can change the hours remaining.

The timer updates automatically and ends at midnight at the start of the birthday date. On that date the card says “Happy birthday!” all day, then counts down to the following year. Months are measured from the current date and time, with short months clamped to their last day.

Hebrew birthdays begin at sunset the evening before. The daytime date and previous evening are listed in setup. This timer counts to midnight on the daytime date; it does not calculate the exact sunset.

A regular Adar birthday uses Adar II in a leap year unless you choose Adar I. Dates entered as Adar I or Adar II use Adar in a regular year. A 30 Adar I birthday uses 30 Shevat in a regular year. If 30 Cheshvan or 30 Kislev is absent, the countdown uses the first day of the next month. These adjustments appear in setup.

English February 29 birthdays can use February 28 or March 1 in other years. Add the original birth year and verify the time of birth to switch between calendars. Switching uses that original date of birth, so each calendar can have a different next birthday. An English birth date stays fixed; for a birth after sunset but before midnight, its matching Hebrew date is the next day. After midnight, both match the same daytime date.
