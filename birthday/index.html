How are the days counted?

Countdowns use calendar days in your device's time zone and update automatically. On the date shown, the card says “Happy birthday!” all day. After that date, it counts down to the following year's birthday.

For Hebrew birthdays, the date shown is the daytime date. The birthday begins at sunset the evening before, which is shown on the card. This is a day countdown, not a timer to the exact sunset.

A regular Adar birthday uses Adar II in a leap year unless you choose Adar I. Dates entered as Adar I or Adar II use Adar in a regular year. A 30 Adar I birthday uses 30 Shevat in a regular year. If 30 Cheshvan or 30 Kislev is absent, the countdown uses the first day of the next month. Each affected card explains the date used.

English February 29 birthdays can use February 28 or March 1 in other years. Adding a birth year shows the age according to the calendar you selected.
